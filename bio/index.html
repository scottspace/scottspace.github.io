The bio204 words, and the one to use if you only take one thing.

Scott Penberthy went to MIT at sixteen and finished a PhD in computer science and artificial intelligence at the University of Washington. In 1992, before most people had heard of the web, he built IBM’s first browser and web server. He was Lou Gerstner’s technical assistant. His team grew from four people to two hundred, and became two internet businesses earning four and thirteen billion dollars annually.

He ran engineering at Photobucket with fifty million users, then moved PwC to the cloud across a hundred and sixty countries. He joined Google Cloud in 2016, when it was under half a billion a year, and started its first applied AI team. Two dozen projects with NASA. Then healthcare, where he landed two billion dollars of business.

Twenty-five years ago he lost his mother to cancer. She asked him to help. He is on the Lustgarten Foundation board, which funded early KRAS work when that target was called undruggable. The first drug to hit it was approved this August. It nearly doubles survival. He advises ClinPGx at Stanford, which matches medicines to a person’s genes.

He works in digital video now, because it teaches a machine how things change. Then he brings it back to cancer.
